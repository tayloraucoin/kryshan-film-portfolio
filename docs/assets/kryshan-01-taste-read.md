## 1. Taste thesis

**Quiet chrome, one saturated cinematic colour that is his, and the work one tap from playing. Warmth and play live in the footage, the voice, the wordmark, and the accent; never in decoration.**

Specific enough to rule things out: no gradient wash, no decorative motion, no photography-led hero, no colour spread across every element, no display face doing the personality's job, no white cells without images, no photos stacked under videos, no template skeleton (hero + three cards). Ground colour is *not* fixed by the thesis; see §4.

### 2.2 The in-place player, seen in four forms
All four were praised; batch 6 assigns one per layout.
1. **Lightbox over the dimmed page** (Roper): the page stays put; overlay with prev/next; cheapest to build; weakest for search unless a detail page sits behind it.
2. **Expand inside the grid with reflow** (McKee): the most playful of the four; needs the grid to be a real layout, not a masonry; reads beautifully with titles-over-thumbnails.
3. **Horizontal strip of full-width players** (Betancourt): a scroll-across reel; matches his "scroll down or across" line; heavy if every player is live, so it needs poster frames and lazy players.
4. **Fast page transition** (Levin): a real project page, reached instantly. Not an overlay, but he experienced it as "instantly watch." Best for sharing and search; costs one navigation.

### 2.3 Lifted devices, now specific
- **Accent phrase inside a headline** (Airview, Kahani): one phrase in the accent colour does the "bold" work without any other element moving. Twice present in his picks. Candidate for the home hero line.
- **Sidebar chrome** (Sanders, Corkle, Biesold): three of the dark/quiet picks put the name, nav, and social links in a left rail and leave the rest to the work. Candidate for one layout.
- **Title + role left, logline right** (Biesold): the per-project block that "doesn't crowd." Candidate for the overlay and detail page.
- **Press quotes under the film in italic serif** (Corkle, a Vancouver peer): exactly where his Rue Morgue and Exclaim! lines belong.
- **Monospace as a personality face** (Betancourt, Petros, Directors Bureau): three sites he rated 5 use a mono for the wordmark or nav. It reads editorial and slightly wry, which suits "wonderfully wrong." A legitimate second face in one kit.
- **Full-bleed colour field** (Directors Bureau): a saturated ground is inside his tolerance for a page or a moment, not for the whole site (it would fail "must never feel crowded" once his frames land on it).

### 2.4 The partial rejections (unchanged, now confirmed on screen)
Sanders: the system, not the photo strips. Boucard: the structure, but "a bit plain," and seen, it is: a template grid with no wordmark presence. McKee: the expansion, not the blank cells. Groshev: the colour, not the dead links or the load time (it loaded to nothing in my session). Geremia: the player, not the density. Kahani: the headline device, not the marketing template.

### 2.5 Self-description versus revealed preference, restated
He says warm (6/7), bold (6/7), playful (6/7), mildly timeless (3/7), and he wrote Creative, Professional, Excellence. What he picked is restrained chrome with one strong colour and confident type. The reconciliation stands, with sharper targets: form from the picks (quiet, sparse, one accent, one tap); aspiration spent in four places:
1. The accent colour, saturated and cinematic (§4).
2. The wordmark: his name, in type, in colour; his own idea, and the device every 5-rated site uses.
3. The naming of the two lives (routed to copy; Petros's line is the model, his words will be his own).
4. The in-place player, which is the site's one deliberate piece of motion.

## 4. Colour

- **Accent: red, saturated, cinematic.** Eight images, the horror heritage (Jack, Bully Solution, Bloodshots), the Jack poster, Betancourt's red wordmark. Red is the lead in two of the three kits. Guardrail: red on black reads "horror site" to a credit-union producer unless the type, spacing, and the corporate frames carry the professionalism; red on cream reads editorial and warm and has no such risk.
- **Amber** is the second family (Wild at Heart, Mad Max, the fishtank, Saturday Night Fever) and pairs naturally with green (In the Mood for Love, Vertigo). That pairing is the third kit.
- **Blue** drops to partner status: a link colour, a division colour, a cool step in a ramp, never the identity.
- **Ground: a real fork, now.** The picks are split seven light, six dark. His own frames decide (§6): a black ground makes a mixed-exposure grid look curated and keeps his night frames from reading as holes; a warm cream ground (Betancourt, Corkle) makes the whole site read warmer and more "him" and gives corporate visitors an easier first impression. Both are built.
- **[I] RGB, tested against the evidence and downgraded.** The three-channels-for-three-roles idea survives only as a tag colour system inside a kit whose identity colour is one of the three. With red this dominant in his imagery, a three-colour identity would dilute it. Batch 4 decides; my expectation is that it lives as role tags in one kit and nowhere else.

## 5. Type

- **A grotesk or condensed grotesk with real weight range** for chrome and wordmark: seen in Sanders (condensed), mjz (condensed uppercase), Corkle (condensed bold), Airview (wide-tracked uppercase), McKee (giant grotesk). This is the family his 5s share.
- **A monospace as the personality face in one kit**, for the wordmark or the nav only: Betancourt, Petros, Directors Bureau. It is the most "authored" move available that is also inside his picks.
- **A serif only for quotes and stories**: Corkle's italic press quotes; Levin's serif wordmark is the exception that proves how quiet it stays. Tested against his real 460-word bio before it is chosen.
- **[M] No default pairings**; licensed for web; checked on a phone on both grounds.
- Wordmark: his name in type, in the accent colour, no logo (02, §1.5). Every 5-rated site has a small confident wordmark and nothing else in the header.

**Grid rule.** Native 16:9, consistent gutters, a uniform title treatment, and one of two grounds: on black, a hairline frame at low opacity so the night frames have an edge; on cream, a slightly heavier frame and a subtle shadow so dark frames don't punch holes. Thumbnails never crop into squares. Vertical work (Shotlister's Instagram spots, if he supplies them) sits at native ratio between 16:9 pieces, as Betancourt does.

**Hover and touch.** Titles over thumbnails (Boucard, Levin), description on hover, tap-to-reveal then tap-to-play on touch. Nothing under a video except the next video or the email.

**Behind-the-scenes photographs.** On set with Ted Danson, with Leatherface, the MPIAA PSA shoot, the concert and multicam rigs, AD-ing in the Yukon, the LaSalle grad: the texture of "works with great teams." Two or three on About, never a gallery; studio-set images held until cleared (02, §10).

**The portrait.** Still only the photo with Ted Danson. About wants him alone and current; he supplies. Until then, name in type plus a poster frame in the hero.

## 8. The three legitimate positions (revised preview for batch 4)

All three: quiet chrome, small wordmark in the accent, one accent with one job, play in one tap. They differ in ground, second colour, and where the personality face goes.

- **A. Red on black.** The largest share of his imagery, the horror heritage, Sanders' and Petros's chrome. Grotesk throughout; the wordmark in red; role tags in type, not colour. The most cinematic. My likely recommendation, because his own frames look most curated on it.
- **B. Red on warm cream.** Betancourt and Corkle, seen: the two light sites he rated highest for feel. Mono wordmark in red on cream; a serif for quotes and stories; the warmest and most "authored" of the three; the easiest first impression for a corporate visitor. The light option is now a real contender, not a courtesy.
- **C. Green and amber on near-black.** Vertigo, In the Mood for Love, the fishtank, Wild at Heart; Airview's accent-phrase device. The least expected; reads editorial and slightly strange, which suits "wonderfully wrong." The one where the bold/playful aspiration is spent in colour temperature rather than in a single hue.

Each gets a pillar card in batch 4 and a full kit in batch 5.
