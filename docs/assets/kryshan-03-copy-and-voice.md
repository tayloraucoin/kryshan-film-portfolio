## 1. The two throughlines (his words, kept)

He wrote them himself in the goals field and they are the spine of the site:

1. **"I am a storyteller, and specialize in bringing unique stories to life. Whether shooting, editing or directing, I can work within any genre, mood, emotional experience and/or time/budget parameters to deliver something cinematic, riveting and a tale well told."**
2. **"I aim to create an environment where all my collaborators can be their best creative selves. Where they feel safe and trusted enough to deliver their best performance, film their best shot… learn from me as a teacher to discover their voice and let it blossom."**

The first is what a producer buys. The second is why they hire him again, and it is the whole case for the teaching strand. Everything on the site is one of these two sentences, said shorter.

A third idea sits underneath both, from the essays, and belongs on About and in the passion work's framing, not on the home page: cinema as **"an empathy machine"** (he credits Ebert), and dark comedy as **"a cry for help"** — realistic characters trapped in extreme situations that society would rather not talk about out loud. His one-sentence self-description from the Berlinale application: *"My favourite subject is human consciousness, and my favourite theme is how quickly one's mind can turn against itself in extreme situations."* That is the most precise thing he has written about his own work, and it explains the pumpkins.

## 2. Voice profile (from his writing)

What he does when he writes, so the site can sound like him and not like a program note.

**Sentence shape.** Long, stacked, semicolon-heavy sentences that carry three or four credits at once ("PSAs for the Directors Guild of Canada, Creative BC, MPIAA and IATSE 669; social media ads for the Shotlister app; TV pitch sizzle reels Promoter and The Shameless Traveller; four episodes of…"). This is the CV habit and it is the thing the site must cut hardest. When he is not listing, his sentences are short and plain ("Made for $25." "Power tools…").

**Humour.** Dry and placed at the end of a sentence, never announced. "Best Death." "Power tools…" "the 'star' was filmed for what he thought was a corporate video." "cameos half from friends and half from strangers, often selected only minutes before appearing on camera." The joke is the last word. Never a wink, never an exclamation mark.

**Specificity.** He names the thing: Canon C70, 35mm in four days, 48 hours, $25, three cameras, a masked crew of five, "one of the windiest days of the year," "the day after their instructor saw the rough cut with Pan's Labyrinth temp music." Specific numbers and props are how he conveys craft without adjectives. The site keeps this.

**Generosity.** He credits everyone: co-writers, co-editors (Alex Barker), the artist he co-directed with (Myk Gordon), the actor whose audition made him rewrite the script (Justine Warrington), the photographer who quoted Bowie on set. "Very collaborative" is demonstrated in how he writes, not claimed.

**Register.** First person, direct, warm, unpretentious. He does not oversell and he does not perform modesty either; he states what happened. He is comfortable with film-nerd references (Kaufman, Lynch, the Coens, Scorsese, Cronenberg, Villeneuve) and with genre vocabulary ("slickly-made," "gore gags," "kid revenge flick") when quoting others.

**Spelling.** Mixed: "favourite" and "colour" in the intake, "favorite" in the 2017 essays. He is a BC filmmaker whose clients are Canadian institutions; **Canadian spelling throughout** (colour, favourite, centre, theatre). He writes "Kick Start" and "Kickstart" both ways; the DGC program is styled **Kickstart**. "5 Rythms" in the intake; the practice is **5Rhythms** [02 §13 Q9].

**What he underclaims.** Thirty-plus years of directing shorts ("over one hundred short film projects" as a one-man band, per the Berlinale essay); the scale of the BTS credit list; the teaching lineage (Frames Film Project for at-risk youth, a founded program, gets one clause). The copy should lift these without inflating them.

**What he overdoes.** Festival lists. Ten festivals in one sentence reads as insecurity to a producer and as noise to a student. Rule: name the award, name at most two festivals, link the rest.

## 3. How he wants to come across, and what to refuse

**Wanted (his words):** creative, collaborative, professional; fun, fast, a natural storyteller; passionate, trustworthy, "works with / can come with great teams." Feel words: Creative · Professional · Excellence.

**Refused (his words):** "someone who just films something without any creativity or initiative"; lazy; passive; crowded; wordy.

**How the copy delivers each, without stating it:**

| He wants to read as | The copy does this | The copy never does this |
|---|---|---|
| Creative | Leads with the strangest true detail of each project | Says "creative" |
| Professional | Precise roles, precise credits, real client names, no typos | Says "professional"; pads with festival lists |
| Collaborative | Credits collaborators by name in project lines; About names the people who shaped his work | Uses "we" for solo work; uses "I" for team work |
| Fast / fun | Short lines; the joke at the end; specific numbers (48 hours, $25) | Exclamation marks; "fun" as an adjective |
| Trustworthy | Everything checkable; NDA'd work stated, not shown; nothing invented | "Award-winning" without the award |
| Not passive | Active verbs: directed, shot, cut, rewrote, founded, taught | "Was responsible for," "helped with," "worked on" |

**Table-stakes words to avoid as claims** (show them instead): passionate, professional, creative, quality, experienced, award-winning, trusted, versatile, dynamic, cinematic (use once, in his own throughline sentence, and nowhere else), storyteller (once, same sentence).

**Words to avoid because of the work he wants less of:** wedding, corporate video, content, videography services, packages, rates.

**Phrases from the intake that should not reach the page as written:** "dark and twisted passion projects" (his private shorthand, fine in a meeting, reads apologetic on a site; the work will say it), "conservative corporate work" (same), "one-man crew" as a headline (undersells; fine as a fact inside the Camera strand), "I'm not lazy" (never).

## 4. The audiences, and what each needs to hear

- **The producer or agency hiring a director.** Needs: range, speed, that he can direct name talent, that he has done broadcast-grade PSAs for the industry's own bodies (DGC, Creative BC, MPIAA, the unions). The proof line: *Just Watch Us* and the sentence he wrote about its result.
- **The producer hiring camera or editing.** Needs: IATSE 669, the Canon C70 kit, the sixty-title EPK list, that he can be a one-person unit, the Leo nomination for editing, the theatre archival/trailer clients (TUTS, Some Assembly, Fringe). Reads role labels literally: "Camera Operator / Editor" is a different hire from "Director."
- **The organisation with a story** (VANDU, RFFC, Courtenay Cohousing, 5Rhythms, Reel Green). Needs to see their kind of work handled with respect and craft, and to feel they will be safe with him. This is where the "environment where collaborators can be their best selves" sentence earns its place.
- **The student or program director.** Needs: where he teaches (VFS, LaSalle), the camps (Mexico, Victoria, Toronto, NWT), the Frames Film Project, one-on-one coaching, and a single clear way to ask. The same email.
- **The festival programmer, press, or peer.** Needs the credits to be exact and the shorts to be watchable in one click. They are served by precision, not by copy.

## 6. The bio: what survives at each length

His 460-word bio is good raw material with a CV's shape: chronological, credit-dense, and it saves the human being for the last line. The site needs three cuts, each made from his sentences, not new ones. This is a cut plan, not a rewrite; the words stay his.

**Short (≈35–45 words, hero and metadata).** One sentence from throughline 1 plus the three roles and the city. The "since 1999 / 27 years" number goes here once it is confirmed (02 §13 #3).

**Medium (≈120–150 words, About opener or a hover card).** Keep, in this order: born and raised in BC; the two fast-film contests as the origin ("Through these events, I met many of my favourite collaborators"); Jack and The Bully Solution with one award each; Glimpse with the Kickstart and VIFF; the name-talent line; the IATSE 669 / one-person-unit line with two or three client names; the Leo nomination; the teaching line with VFS and LaSalle; the interests line, verbatim, because it is the only sentence in the bio that is about him rather than his credits.

**Long (his full bio, lightly edited).** Everything, with these edits: cut the ten-festival lists to two festivals and a link; move "Outside of the industry…" up to the second paragraph so the person appears before the résumé; add one sentence from the essays about *why* (the empathy machine / the cry for help), because a producer hiring a director wants to know what he is for; fix the fact conflicts once answered.

**What the About page adds beyond the bio (from the essays; his own words, to be approved):**
- The Glimpse anecdote: an actor's audition so strong he rewrote the script around her. This is throughline 2 in one story and is worth more than any adjective.
- The dark-comedy thesis, one sentence.
- The Marianne Williamson quote he uploaded as inspiration is *not* for the page (it is not his and it is well-worn); its idea is already in his own sentence about collaborators.

## 9. Rules for anyone writing on this site

1. His words first; edit down, do not rewrite up.
2. One award, two festivals, link the rest.
3. Roles are precise and literal.
4. Names he may state are stated; nothing NDA'd is shown or implied by a thumbnail.
5. The joke is the last word, or there is no joke.
6. No table-stakes adjectives; prove them.
7. Canadian spelling; no exclamation marks; no emoji.
8. Every number and date traces to 02 §8 or §13, or waits.
9. Nothing about the events side of his life beyond his own interests sentence.
10. Every page ends with the email.
